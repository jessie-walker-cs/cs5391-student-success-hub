Beyond CS 5391

Make your work part of your professional portfolio.

The project should produce evidence you can discuss in an interview: the problem, your decisions, your code, your tests, and what changed through feedback. Reuse is subject to course rules, team permissions and applicable licensing.

01

Track what you contribute

Use commits, issue assignments, pull requests, design notes, test cases and sprint demonstrations to document work you actually performed. Keep a short personal contribution log.

02

Confirm what may be public

During the course, keep graded work in instructor-approved repositories. Before publishing any files on a personal account, obtain permission consistent with the syllabus, team agreements and rights of other contributors.

03

Prepare a clean portfolio copy

Remove personal information, private issues, real records, grades, peer feedback, credentials and unpublished team material. Use your own work, appropriately attributed excerpts, or an approved fork.

04

Create your own repository

After authorization, create a repository on your GitHub account. For a personal project website you can use username.github.io; for an individual project you can use any appropriate repository name.

05

Write a case-study README

Explain the real-world problem, your role, project constraints, architecture, technical contributions, testing approach, images or diagrams you own, and lessons learned. Credit collaborators fairly.

06

Publish and maintain

Enable GitHub Pages in your own repository if desired, check all public links and remove any sensitive metadata. Add a demo with fictional data, maintain a license appropriate to what you own, and continue developing it after the term.

What a good portfolio case study includes

  • Project context and the problem you worked to solve
  • Your specific role and the features you implemented
  • Requirements, design decisions and diagrams you may share
  • Selected commits, pull requests and test evidence
  • Screenshots of sanitized demo data or a permitted live demo
  • Tradeoffs, limitations, revisions and what you learned
  • Names or links acknowledging collaborators, where appropriate

How to move work to your own GitHub

  1. Ask the instructor for permission to publish particular course materials after assessment.
  2. Check any repository license and the contributions of teammates. Public visibility by itself does not grant permission to copy code.
  3. If permitted, fork the repository or copy only your own approved files and preserve author attributions and license notices.
  4. Alternatively, create a new demonstration implementation based on your personal learning, without copying teammates' restricted material.
  5. Push sanitized files, write a README, then configure GitHub Pages in Settings → Pages on your own repository.
